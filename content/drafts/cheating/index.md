What can only truly be described as an "epidemic of cheaters" uniquely exists in the world of competitive online gaming.
Due to anonymity of the perpetrators, alongside the commodity of pseudonyms, a strangely large portion of active players across any given title aren't really there to play the game;
swathes of real people are "playing" on the tenth hijacked account they purchased with real money,
injecting their three-to-four-figure monthy cheat SaaS subsciption into the game,
just to feel the immense power of: ...ruining other people's competitive spirit.

Given how unprofitable playing videogames is, you might expect more players to actually play the game for the love of the game.
Yet the sheer accessibility of cheating combined with zero repercussions for getting caught foster an environment where many can't quite push down their feelings of inadequacy by using the potential future shame of being labelled a cheater.

Most interesting of all is the real, scale economy that exists surrounding the tooling and resources needed to cheat in a game.
Cheat providers distribute competing software and hardware products to cheat harder or stealthier than their peers.
Account providers hijack or farm large quantities of accounts that appear as legitimate players to sell to players who just got banned and need a new account.
Tools are sold to bypass anti-cheat and anti-tamper software, re-roll identifying information, and obscure identities of the tool users.
Entire communities exist solely to discuss the latest cheat and tool releases, flex their "achievements", theory craft creative approaches to cheating, and providing the resources newbies need to install their first P2C.
And in a surprising turn of events, some of these communities even have self-run gamemodes and tournaments where anything goes, which itself is a real competitive meta-game of sorts.

Above moral qualms, I find the entire situation deeply fascinating.
There is much to learn about human psychology through these players' behaviours,
economics classes that *should* be based on the cheating micro-economy,
game theory to develop from learnings of actual cheaters,
and an entire parallel world to explore of how others enjoy the same dish by radically different methods of consumption.
